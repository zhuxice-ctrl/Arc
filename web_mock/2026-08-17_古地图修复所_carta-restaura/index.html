Methodology

The Restoration Process

Six deliberate stages, guided by both scientific rigor and the patience of a craft that has not changed in five hundred years.

Assessment & Documentation

Every map begins with a full condition report — multispectral imaging, fiber analysis, and ink solubility tests establish a complete picture of the artifact's state before a single brush touches it.

I
II

Dry Cleaning

Surface grime is lifted with soft vulcanized rubber sponges and fine squirrel-hair brushes. For more stubborn accretions, we use micro-suction and enzyme gels tailored to the specific soil.

Wet Treatment & Deacidification

A controlled aqueous bath in calcium-magnesium solution neutralizes acids and draws out soluble discoloration. Water-sensitive media is protected with a reversible barrier film.

III
IV

Mending & Infill

Tears are aligned and reinforced on the verso with toned Japanese tissue and wheat starch paste. Losses receive sympathetic infills that support the structure without mimicking original material.

Inpainting & Retouching

Where the original image is interrupted by losses, our inpainting specialists work under magnification with reversible pigments — visible only as coherent image from arm's length.

V
VI

Encapsulation & Housing

The final step is preservation: archival mounting, UV-filtering acrylic glazing, and a custom-built drop-spine box. Every map leaves with a full condition record for future conservators.

The Atelier

Where time is both patient and precious.

Carta Restaura was founded in a quiet studio off the Arno, where restorers have tended to paper and ink since 2014. What began as a two-person workshop has grown into eleven master artisans, each trained in the traditions of the Florentine Opificio and the Venetian conservatories.

We do not rush. A 16th-century chart that took a cartographer months to draw deserves at least as much care to preserve. Our clients — museums, archives, private collectors, and libraries across three continents — know this. They come to us not for speed, but for the certainty that when a map leaves our studio, it is ready for the next five hundred years.

— Elena Vasari, Founding Restorer
