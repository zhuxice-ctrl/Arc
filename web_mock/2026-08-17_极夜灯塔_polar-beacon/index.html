Kjerag Watch Light

Engineering · 灯器

The Fresnel Lens

A stepped lens that captures light from a single source and projects it into a narrow beam. Invented in 1822, it remains the heart of every lighthouse.

First-order
Rotating Lens

The Kjerag lantern room houses a first-order Fresnel lens with 8 panels, rotating once every 20 seconds on a bed of 1,200 kg of mercury. A single 1000W incandescent lamp produces a beam visible up to 24 nautical miles.

Below the lens sits the clockwork mechanism — a descending weight that must be wound every three hours by the keeper. A tradition unchanged for over a century.

History · 纪年

A Century of Light

From whale-oil lamps to electrified beacons — a century of keeping the polar coast illuminated through war, automation, and the long dark.

1903 · 建造

Kjerag Watch Light lit for the first time

Three keepers and their families move into the station. The lens is lit by a five-wick kerosene lamp producing 120,000 candlepower.

1926 · 扩建

Diaphone fog horn installed

A compressed-air diaphone replaces the old explosive fog signal. Its two-tone blast can be distinguished from all other stations within 60 km.

1942 · 战争

The dark year of the occupation

The light is extinguished for 11 months. Keepers continue to maintain the lens in secret, polishing the prisms by candlelight every evening.

1958 · 电气化

Electric lamp replaces kerosene

A 1000W incandescent lamp is installed. Output jumps to 1.2 million candlepower. The old clockwork remains — it still rotates the lens.

1989 · 自动化

The last keeper departs

After 86 years of continuous human presence, the station becomes fully automated. The keeper's quarters are preserved as a museum.

2025 · 当下

Archive project begins

Polar Beacon Archive is founded to digitize, preserve, and share the stories of 47 lighthouses along the polar rim before they fade.
