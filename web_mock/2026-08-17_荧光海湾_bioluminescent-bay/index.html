The Chemistry

Cold light, warm science.

1

The Luciferin Reaction

Light is born when luciferin reacts with oxygen in the presence of the enzyme luciferase. Unlike incandescence, nearly 100% of the energy becomes light — no heat is wasted.

2

Why Blue-Green?

Blue-green light (470–510 nm) travels farthest through seawater. Evolution has tuned bioluminescence to the wavelengths that move best through the medium where it matters most.

3

The Burglar Alarm Hypothesis

When a predator disturbs glowing plankton, the flash attracts larger predators — a "burglar alarm" that turns the hunter into the hunted. One of the ocean's cleverest defense strategies.

4

Circadian Rhythm of Glow

Dinoflagellates glow only at night, following a 24-hour internal clock. The chemicals recharge during the day via photosynthesis — making midnight the brightest hour of all.

Field Guide

How to see the glow — respectfully.

Bioluminescent bays are fragile ecosystems. Follow these guidelines to witness the magic without disturbing it.

TIP 01

Go on a new moon

The darker the sky, the brighter the glow. Aim for nights within 3 days of the new moon. Avoid full moons — moonlight drowns out the bioluminescence entirely.

TIP 02

Give your eyes 20 minutes

Human eyes need time to fully dark-adapt. Avoid all white light — phones, flashlights, even glow sticks ruin night vision. Use red light if you must see something.

TIP 03

Look for the right conditions

Calm water after a warm day usually means the best glow. Heavy rain dilutes the bay; strong wind churns it up. Check local plankton bloom reports.

TIP 04

Choose eco-certified tours

Many bays are protected reserves. Book with licensed, low-impact operators — no motor boats, no sunscreen in the water, no touching the mangroves.

TIP 05

Make waves — gently

The glow needs motion to trigger. Kick your feet, swirl your hand, drag your fingers through the surface. But never scoop organisms out of the water — they die within minutes in open air.

TIP 06

Photograph it right

Use a long exposure (4–15 seconds), high ISO (1600–6400), and a steady surface. Phone night modes work surprisingly well. Flash will kill the glow and annoy everyone around you.
